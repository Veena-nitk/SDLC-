Title: Conversational Voice AI, Biometric Authentication, and Next-Gen FinTech UX Standards
Scope: Voice-driven banking, natural language transaction assistants, biometric identity verification (FIDO2 / WebAuthn), and consumer wallet interactions.
Standards & Regulatory References: FIDO Alliance FIDO2 / WebAuthn, ISO/IEC 19794-5 (Biometric Face Image Data), GDPR Article 9 (Special Categories of Data - Biometrics), NIST SP 800-63B (Digital Identity Guidelines - AAL3).

Section 1: Conversational Dialogue Management & Natural Language Understanding
1. Natural language voice queries in financial transactions (e.g., "Send $50 to Alice from checking", "What was my largest debit this week?") contain high linguistic variance, background acoustic noise, accent heterogeneity, and intent ambiguity.
2. The dialogue state tracker must support multi-turn disambiguation, acoustic confidence scoring, and explicit confirmation prompts for monetary transfers exceeding $25.
3. Fallback mechanisms: If automated intent recognition confidence falls below 0.85, the system must gracefully offer visual card choices or numeric confirmation.

Section 2: Biometric Custody & Privacy by Design
1. Facial biometric vectors and vocal acoustic prints must never leave the user's secure enclave on the mobile device in raw unencrypted form.
2. The platform authenticates transactions using asymmetric cryptographic key-pairs bound to the device's hardware TPM (Trusted Platform Module) per FIDO2 standards.
3. Under GDPR Article 9 and CCPA, explicit affirmative biometric consent is required, accompanied by an alternative non-biometric authentication path (alphanumeric password + SMS/Authenticator TOTP).

Section 3: SDLC Methodology Alignment (Why Prototyping is Essential)
1. Extreme interaction ambiguity: Voice and gesture-driven financial interfaces represent completely novel consumer experiences. Users cannot describe ideal conversational flows through text specifications.
2. Throwaway UI/UX prototyping: The team must rapidly build, test, and discard iterative voice interaction mockups and screen micro-animations with real user focus groups.
3. Rapid learning cycles: Testing user error tolerance, wake-word responsiveness, and acoustic latency in disposable prototypes prevents premature investment in complex backend microservices architectures before the interaction paradigm is validated.
